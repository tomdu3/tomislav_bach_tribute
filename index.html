Johann Sebastian Bach

Composer • Organist • Baroque Master

About

Johann Sebastian Bach is a composer of the Baroque era, the most celebrated member of a large family of north German musicians. Although he was admired by his contemporaries primarily as an outstanding harpsichordist, organist, and expert on organ building, Bach is now generally regarded as one of the greatest composers of all time.

Bio

  • Born 31 March, 1685 in Eisenach, Germany
  • Died 28 July, 1750 in Leipzig, Germany.
  • Influence: foundation for much of classical harmony and counterpoint

Famous Works

  1. The Well-Tempered Clavier (Book I & II) – A collection of 48 preludes and fugues exploring all major and minor keys.
  2. Brandenburg Concertos (Nos. 1-6) – A set of six concertos showcasing Baroque orchestral mastery.
  3. Mass in B Minor – A monumental choral work considered one of the greatest compositions in Western music.
  4. St. Matthew Passion – A large-scale sacred oratorio depicting the Passion of Christ.
  5. Goldberg Variations – An intricate set of 30 variations for harpsichord.
  6. Toccata and Fugue in D minor – One of Bach’s most famous organ compositions, often associated with dramatic moments.
  7. Art of Fugue (Die Kunst der Fuge) – An exploration of contrapuntal composition, left unfinished at his death.
  8. The Christmas Oratorio – A collection of six cantatas celebrating the Nativity.
  9. Suite No. 3 in D major for Solo Cello – Renowned for its expressive and technical brilliance.
  10. Cello Suites (Nos. 1-6) – A set of six suites for solo cello, fundamental to cello repertoire.
